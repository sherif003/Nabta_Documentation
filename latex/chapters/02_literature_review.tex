\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related_work}

Artificial Intelligence has become one of the most influential technologies in modern agriculture, supporting farmers in disease diagnosis, crop management, irrigation planning, and intelligent decision-making. Recent advances in computer vision, machine learning, explainable artificial intelligence, and large language models have significantly improved the accuracy and accessibility of smart agriculture systems.

Plant disease diagnosis has evolved from traditional manual inspection toward automated computer vision systems capable of recognizing disease symptoms directly from plant images. Early studies, including Mohanty et al.~\cite{mohanty2016}, demonstrated that Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) could achieve high classification accuracy on controlled datasets such as PlantVillage. Although these approaches showed promising results, they generally relied on single-stage image classification and were less effective when applied to real agricultural environments containing complex backgrounds, varying illumination, multiple leaves, and occlusions.

Subsequent research introduced object detection models such as the YOLO family to localize diseased plant regions before classification. More recently, semantic segmentation architectures such as U-Net have been employed to isolate leaf regions at the pixel level, reducing background noise and improving classification robustness. Explainable Artificial Intelligence techniques, particularly Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (Grad-CAM), have also been integrated to provide visual explanations that improve user trust by highlighting the image regions responsible for model predictions.

Beyond plant disease diagnosis, machine learning has been widely adopted for precision agriculture applications. Crop recommendation systems analyze soil nutrients, soil characteristics, weather conditions, and environmental factors to identify the most suitable crops for cultivation. Random Forest classifiers have demonstrated strong predictive performance due to their robustness, ability to model nonlinear relationships, and resistance to overfitting. Similarly, irrigation recommendation systems utilize climatic variables, soil moisture, and crop information to determine irrigation requirements and estimate crop water demand. Regression models are commonly employed to estimate the required irrigation water, while classification models determine whether irrigation is necessary under current environmental conditions.

Large Language Models (LLMs) have recently emerged as intelligent assistants capable of providing natural-language agricultural consultation. When combined with Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG), these systems retrieve domain-specific agricultural knowledge before generating responses, thereby reducing hallucinations and improving the factual reliability of recommendations.

Despite these advances, most existing solutions focus on only one agricultural task, such as disease classification, irrigation scheduling, or conversational assistance. Few systems integrate computer vision, machine learning, explainable AI, and conversational intelligence within a single production-ready platform.

The proposed \nabta platform addresses this limitation by combining multiple artificial intelligence technologies into a unified smart agriculture ecosystem. The platform integrates YOLOv8 object detection, U-Net semantic segmentation, CNN-based disease classification, Grad-CAM explainability, Random Forest models for crop recommendation and smart irrigation, and a Cohere-powered Retrieval-Augmented Generation assistant. By integrating these complementary technologies within a scalable web-based architecture, NABTA provides comprehensive AI-powered decision support for modern precision agriculture.

\section{Overview}
\label{sec:analysis_overview}

System analysis represents the foundation of software engineering by transforming user needs and domain knowledge into a structured set of functional and non-functional requirements that guide the subsequent design, implementation, testing, and deployment phases.

This chapter presents the complete system analysis of the NABTA platform. It identifies the project's stakeholders and user roles, defines the functional and non-functional requirements, specifies the system use cases, and introduces representative test cases used to verify the implemented functionalities. Particular emphasis is placed on the integration of multiple artificial intelligence services, including plant disease diagnosis, crop recommendation, irrigation recommendation, historical analytics, and AI-assisted agricultural consultation.

The requirements were derived through literature review, agricultural domain analysis, user-centered design principles, and continuous discussions with the project supervisor throughout the project lifecycle. The resulting specification served as the primary reference for the implementation of the proposed smart agriculture platform.

\section{User Roles}
\label{sec:analysis_roles}

The NABTA platform defines three user roles with different permissions and levels of access.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Free-Tier User}
\label{subsec:free_user}

The Free-Tier User represents the default access level available to all registered users. Free-tier users can upload plant images for disease analysis, receive crop and irrigation recommendations, access the AI agricultural assistant within predefined daily limits, and review recent analysis history. They also have access to the interactive analytics dashboard and may download diagnosis reports in PDF format.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Pro-Tier User}
\label{subsec:pro_user}

Pro-tier users obtain unrestricted access to all intelligent services through a subscription-based plan. Additional privileges include unlimited daily analyses, extended chatbot usage, advanced dashboard analytics, long-term history storage, custom dataset upload for future model training, and priority access to newly released AI features.

%------------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{System Administrator}
\label{subsec:admin_user}

System administrators possess elevated privileges for platform management. Their responsibilities include monitoring system performance, managing user accounts, maintaining AI models, supervising the knowledge base used by the RAG pipeline, and ensuring the security, reliability, and availability of all deployed services.

\section{Chapter Summary}
\label{sec:chapter2_summary}

This chapter reviewed the literature related to artificial intelligence applications in smart agriculture and analyzed the functional requirements of the proposed NABTA platform. The stakeholder groups, user roles, functional requirements, non-functional requirements, use cases, and representative test cases were identified and documented.

The presented analysis established the foundation for the system architecture and implementation discussed in the following chapters. By integrating computer vision, machine learning, explainable artificial intelligence, and large language models within a unified software platform, the proposed system addresses multiple agricultural challenges, including plant disease diagnosis, crop recommendation, irrigation recommendation, and intelligent agricultural consultation.
