\section{Overview}
\label{sec:results_overview}

This chapter presents the quantitative evaluation results for each component model within the \nabta pipeline, followed by an end-to-end system performance assessment and a comparative discussion relative to relevant prior work. All results reported in this chapter are computed on the held-out test partitions that were isolated prior to any training activity and never influenced model selection or hyperparameter tuning decisions.

  \parbox{\textwidth}{\footnotesize\vspace{3pt}* Results reported on the laboratory-condition PlantVillage dataset; direct comparison with field-image performance should therefore be interpreted cautiously.}

The \nabta classification accuracy of 96.4\% across 102 classes on a mixed real-world dataset represents a more demanding benchmark than the laboratory-condition PlantVillage-only results reported by preceding studies. The substantially larger class space (102 vs.\ 25--38) and the inclusion of outdoor, variable-lighting images from PlantDoc and regional datasets make the \nabta evaluation significantly more challenging. The multi-stage pipeline---wherein YOLOv8 and U-Net preprocessing remove background noise before classification---is the primary factor enabling the CNN to maintain high accuracy under these more realistic conditions.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

The results demonstrate that the \nabta pipeline architecture successfully achieves its design objectives. The 96.4\% classification accuracy across 102 classes confirms that the sequential ROI extraction and segmentation preprocessing stages provide a measurable benefit over single-stage classifiers operating on raw field images. The sub-400ms end-to-end latency validates the choice of \fastapi with asynchronous dispatch as the serving framework.

The predominance of network transmission time in the latency breakdown (66.4\%) suggests that further overall latency reductions are most effectively pursued through edge deployment strategies---specifically, model quantisation for on-device inference---rather than further server-side optimisation. This observation directly motivates the TensorFlow Lite edge deployment roadmap item described in \chapref{chap:conclusion}.

The highest per-class error rates were observed on classes with visually similar symptom presentations across different host species---for example, distinguishing powdery mildew on cucumber from powdery mildew on mango, where the lesion morphology is similar but the host tissue texture differs. Targeted data collection for such confusable class pairs is identified as a priority for future dataset expansion.
